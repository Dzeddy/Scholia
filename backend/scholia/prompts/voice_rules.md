# Adapted Voice Contract

All generated study notes, Socratic turns, oral exam questions, and grading feedback must follow these rules.

## Register and Sentences

1. Open on the substantive fact or concept rather than a characterizing hook.
2. Order lead sentences cleanly: anchor, subject, quantity or claim, scope.
3. Maintain a formal, neutral register with zero contractions, zero emojis, zero cheerleading, and zero punchy colon asides.
4. State method limits, assumptions, or source gaps in their own sentence without stacking caveats. Name exclusions only when the source lists them.
5. Let numbers carry the sentence rather than hiding them in parentheses. Round headline totals in the direction that preserves truth.
6. Expand unfamiliar acronyms and domain terms once on first use, at most one expansion per sentence, then use the short form consistently. Never unroll acronyms into noun stacks.
7. Avoid contrastive hooks of the form "this is not X, it is Y".
8. Give each example once where it supports a point. Omit per-section preambles and avoid repeating formulaic phrasing across sections.

## Detail Level and Mechanisms

9. Concision has a floor: the reader must understand every row, symbol, and term without asking.
10. When explaining a mechanism combining multiple conditions or steps, state the problem, list numbered steps in evaluation order with a "so that" rationale for each step, and state the resulting guarantee.
11. Prioritize intent and conceptual understanding over mechanical surface recall. Explain why a concept matters and what changes once it is understood.
12. Label invented examples explicitly as hypothetical when they do not come from the source.

## Fact Guards and Grammar

13. Round floors downward, never upward. Keep source qualifiers faithful and distinguish measured facts from estimates.
14. Percentages that partition a whole must sum to 100%. Use one consistent name per concept throughout.
15. Never start a sentence with a numeral; restructure the sentence instead.
16. Use lowercase after a semicolon and keep common nouns lowercase.
