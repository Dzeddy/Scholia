# Study Notes Pedagogy Contract

Write notes that teach the future reader six months later, enabling full conceptual reconstruction rather than surface recognition.

## Core Principles

1. **Explain rather than record.** Reconstruct each load-bearing idea in plain language. Translate domain jargon on first use and make implicit reasoning explicit.
2. **Anchor abstraction in concrete examples.** Pair every core claim with at least one specific example from the source or an explicit hypothetical example from another domain. Give multiple examples for ideas that need to generalize across domains.
3. **Force active engagement on rereading.** Include probing self-quiz prompts, boundary comparisons, and failure conditions that force the reader back into the reasoning.
4. **Match depth to substance.** Do not artificially compress complex or counterintuitive ideas. Dwell on surprising claims where intuition is most likely to drift back to conventional assumptions.

## Required Components (Adapt to Material)

- **TL;DR (drafted last):** Two to four sentences capturing the load-bearing takeaway after the entire chapter is worked out.
- **Why this matters:** What changes operationally or conceptually when this material is understood; what mistakes it prevents.
- **Key ideas:** Load-bearing concepts (typically 3 to 7, or more for dense chapters). For each concept include:
  - Plain-language reconstruction of the mechanism
  - Concrete worked example (labeled hypothetical if not from the source)
  - Justification or author reasoning moves
  - Downstream implications (what follows, what this changes)
  - Connections to prerequisite or adjacent ideas
- **Argument structure (or narrative craft for non-argumentative works):** Claim -> evidence -> counterargument addressed -> conclusion.
- **Connections:** Explicit mapping to prior chapters, prerequisite concepts, or contrasting frameworks.
- **Open questions and confusions:** Explicit gaps, weak assumptions, or unverified claims.
- **Self-quiz prompts:** Four to eight probing questions testing failure cases, mechanisms, and transfer rather than rote summary.

## Failure Modes to Avoid

- Compressed transcripts mirroring section headers without explaining mechanisms.
- Aesthetic outlines with nested headings that state no substance.
- Unannotated quote collections.
- Summaries that paper over confusions.
- Equal weight across trivial and load-bearing claims.
- Artificially terse notes that leave future readers unable to reconstruct understanding.
